# casefiles

Independent case studies I write for my own sake.

I'm a computer science student. Cybersecurity and geopolitics are hobbies of mine, and this repository is where I think out loud about incidents that sit at the intersection of the two — events I find interesting enough to work through carefully, from the perspectives those fields give me.

## What these are, and what they aren't

Everything here is an **independent case study**, written to apply what I'm learning and to clarify my own thinking. Nothing in this repository has been peer-reviewed, commissioned, or published anywhere else — this is simply the way I happen to view a particular incident on the day I wrote the piece.

To be clear about what these are not:

- They are **not whitepapers**.
- They are **not policy briefings**.
- They are **not journalism** or reporting.
- They are **not professional, legal, or security advice**.

I cite sources because I want the arguments to be checkable, not because I'm claiming any special access or authority. Treat these as the notebook of a hobbyist, not the output of an institution.

## Method

Each case study tries to do three things:

1. **State the incident** in plain terms, with dates and primary sources.
2. **Apply a framework** — strategic, technical, legal, or economic — that helps make sense of what is actually happening.
3. **Draw a conclusion** that follows from the sources cited, and nothing more.

If I later find I've got something wrong, I correct it in a subsequent commit rather than silently editing the original. The git history is part of the record.
